# DESIGN.md: ChamberWatch

This is the UI style file: how the web app in `frontend/` looks and behaves. The system design (data, detectors, API, storage) is [docs/DESIGN.md](docs/DESIGN.md).

## The look in one sentence
Cassette: a lab notebook in true ink on warm paper, where every wafer is drawn as a disc, one red means out of range (on the ink masthead it also underlines the page in view and dots the wordmark), and each section is a numbered figure whose serif title and one-line answer sit over the chart that proves it.

## Why it changed again (2026-11-20)
The first style (2026-10, "Attention first") put every section in a soft rounded card, and every page looked like the same generic dashboard. The lab-notebook style that replaced it kept the answer-first rule but still read as plain: the wafer grid was rows of tan boxes, beige sat on beige, the charts and tables looked like library defaults, and there was too much text, with a raw file name as the run page's title. Cassette keeps the notebook and makes it bolder: black ink, a solid ink masthead, one saturated red, large mono numerals, and wafers as discs instead of boxes.

## Colors
Light is warm paper; dark is the same notebook at night, the same tokens swapped under `prefers-color-scheme: dark` (unless the theme button picked light) and under `[data-theme='dark']`.

Charts are ink. A second series is dashed with hollow points, never a second hue. Red marks state, so it never decorates.

## Type
- Newsreader 600 for the `h1`, which states the page's answer (60px, 36px on a phone, at most 17 characters wide), for figure titles (`h2`, 28px, 24px on a phone) and for lot names in tables (20px).
- IBM Plex Sans for text and controls: 15px body, 20px lead under a page's answer (17px on a phone, and 17px under the run and lot pages' longer leads), 17px figure answers, 13px captions in `--ink-2`.
- IBM Plex Mono for figure numbers, column heads, ticks, scores, counts and the values at the ends of chart lines. A score or z that rounds to zero prints 0.0, never -0.0. Big numerals (28px; 72px for the verdict) carry the key numbers, never inside a box. A number set in mono never breaks from its unit, and in a sentence (a headline, a lead, a figure's answer) `tieUnits` in `format.ts` puts a no-break space between a number and its unit (`s`, `µm`, `sd`, `sccm`).
- A score or a fitted z printed beside its limit takes decimals, up to four, until it no longer reads as the limit: 5.996 under a limit of 6.0, never 6.0 (`formatAgainstLimit` in `format.ts`). A disc keeps its four characters; its fill already says which side of the limit it is on.
- A time axis prints its unit with its last tick, "700 s", never as a loose letter beside it, and that label ends on its mark where, centred, it would pass the plot's end (`timeAxisLabels` in `charts/scale.ts`).
- One scale, as tokens named for their size: `--text-12` / 13 / 15 / 17 / 20 / 24 / 28 / 36 / 60 / 72. Every `font-size` in the stylesheet is one of them; nothing renders below 12px. Fonts load from Google Fonts in `frontend/index.html`.

## Figures
- A section that shows data is a figure: a 2px ink rule, then `FIG. n`, the serif title and an optional control (such as the Source switch) on one line, then one sentence that answers it, then the chart or table. No background, no radius, no shadow.
- The number is a CSS counter on the figure, in page order, so a figure that is absent leaves no gap. Small panels with no data of their own take the rule and the title without a number.
- Headlines and answers state facts in plain words. Definitions and methods sit in closed disclosures.
- Charts label marks directly where they can; a key names only what its chart holds.

## Shared pieces (`frontend/src/components/` unless named)
- `Figure`: the rule, the counted label, the `h2`, an optional `control`, an optional `answer`, then children. `numbered={false}` for small panels; `busy` while it holds a placeholder. A `.figure-note` sits close under the answer, or 0px under the title when there is none.
- `Disc`: a wafer. In range is a hollow ring, out of range a solid red disc that may print its score, not scored or not aligned a dashed ring. Sizes `map` 46px (38px at 900px and under, 34px at 760px and under, and on a phone the map's pitch less 2px: 24px from 362px, about 20px at 320px), `strip` 34px, `here` 48px, `ledger` 26px, `cluster` 32px, `mini` 18px on a 26 by 32px target, `key` 34px. A link with `to`, a name with `label`, a centre dot with `baseline`, the ink halo and `aria-current` with `current`.
- `ScoreLine`: a score on a line from 0 to a stated end: a 1px axis, a 2px ink tick at the limit, a red bar and dot out of range, a hollow ring on a stem in range, an arrow past the end and a "+" on the end's tick. `size="verdict"` and `ticks` give the run page's large version; a limit within a fifth of 0 sets its label from the tick rightward and drops the 0, so the two never overlap. One column of lines shares one end, from `scoreLineEnd` in `charts/scale.ts`, and one right margin: when any line in a table or list runs past its end, every line there leaves room for the arrow, so the limit ticks stand at one x. An in-range ring never sits on or across the limit: it stops 2px short of the tick, however close its score, since the number beside it carries the size (`.under` in the stylesheet, shared with the lineup). A red dot is never moved.
- `baselinePrefix` in `baseline.ts`: N when every lot keeps its baseline runs in its first N positions. Then one bracket marks them over those columns; otherwise each baseline disc carries a centre dot and the key says so.
- Ledger tables: mono 12px uppercase column heads over a 2px ink rule, hairline rows, numbers right-aligned in mono. A column of readings whose values all sit within a factor of ten of each other prints them to one count of decimals, so the decimal points line up (0.001590 over 0.001588); one that spreads wider prints each at four significant digits, since lining up 573.3 and 0.0007783 would invent digits (`alignedFormat` in `format.ts`; zeros and missing values do not count). A z prints with `formatZ` in every table: one decimal under 100, whole from there. Tags are words in mono capitals: ink for a role, `--drift` for a warning.
- `Segmented` is a text switch: a row of words, the one in effect 600 ink over a 2px ink underline. Tabs are the same with a 3px underline and a mono count. `Icon` keeps only functional icons (theme, retry).
- `LotGrid` (`charts/`) draws the Runs page's cassette map: lots as rows of discs on a rail, one tab stop, with arrows, Home and End to move.
- `LotStrip`: one lot as a row of `strip` discs, each a link to its run and printing its wafer number, under one bracket or with centre dots by the baseline rule. Feed it `lotStrip(rows, run)` from `run.ts` on the run page (that wafer drawn `here` with a THIS WAFER label) or `lotWafers(rows, lotId)` on a lot's page (no wafer marked). A score goes in the disc's name, never in its print, so one number means one thing along the row. `LotStripPlaceholder` holds its height while the runs load.
- `PositionChart` (`charts/`): values by wafer position in ink. `baseline={n}` shades positions 1 to n in `--paper-2` under `zoneLabel` (BASELINE by default; FIRST 3 on the depth charts and on a lot's page, where the shaded wafers are the reference by position, not the baseline runs); `labelEnds` prints each line's last value at its end in mono 17px with the series name under it, pushed apart by `spreadLabels` in `charts/scale.ts`; `peak` annotates the first line's highest point. A band, a fitted line and its projection are optional. The key draws each series as the chart does: a line for a connected series, a dot for one drawn as dots alone. The pointer or the arrow keys read one wafer at a time; `details` adds rows to the readout, and a visually hidden live region, mounted from the start, reads the same rows aloud.
- `Stat`: a mono label over a 28px mono numeral (`compact` for a name at 17px), under a hairline. Rows of them carry the wafer page's facts and the Live page's readings.
- `ErrorState`, `EmptySource` and `AnswerSkeleton` (`States.tsx`): what the API said, in a figure's place, with Try again; where a source has no wafers yet, how that source fills (with "Open the Live page" for Live) and no title, since the page's `h1` already says it is empty, the same on the Runs and Lots pages; and a page's answer held at its own height while its data loads.
- `NotFound` and `PageFailure` (`pages/NotFound.tsx`): an address with no page, and a run, lot or wafer page whose record did not load. When the API answers 404, `PageFailure` shows the not-found pattern with its own headline and way back ("There is no run 9999." and "Back to the runs"; "There is no lot 99." and "Back to the lots"), since Try again cannot help; any other failure keeps `ErrorState` under a visually hidden `h1`. Copy that differs by source (where the wafers come from, how an empty source fills) lives in `sources.ts`.

## The Runs page
- The `h1` counts the out-of-range runs, the count in `--signal` and plain text to a screen reader ("1 of 96 runs is out of range, in lot 8." for one); the lead names the lot to start with. A source where nothing is scored says so ("None of the 96 runs has been scored yet."), never that none is out of range, and the clean-lots line calls such lots "none scored".
- Fig. 1, the cassette map, with the Source switch in its head, usable while the map shows its placeholder. Each lot ends in its Out count, a mono numeral at 28px (20px at 760px and under, 17px on a phone); a zero is 20px in `--ink-3` (15px on a phone), larger than the Lots table's zero because it sits beside 46px discs. Then text tabs: Needs a look (Fig. 2, which channel left first, as red discs clustered by lot; Fig. 3, the out-of-range runs as one ledger on a shared score line and a shared time line; Fig. 4, drift along the lots), All runs and In range (the same ledger style, sortable, filtered by lot: Wafer, State, How unusual, First channel, Left at, Excursions, Label, the wafer links underlined like every link; on a phone a row with no first channel and no time hides both). On a phone the wafer's name takes a fixed 128px column (88px on a lot's page, where the name is "Wafer 5" alone), so the discs line up row to row; the state's words wrap under the disc, and a public file name breaks after an underscore. Every phone ledger, Fig. 3 included, names its fields one way: the cell's `data-label`, printed before the value in the sans face in `--ink-3`. The clean lots take one sentence and the page's words sit in a closed disclosure.

## The run page
- The `h1` is a sentence from the run's state, from `runHeadline` in `run.ts`: "Wafer 5 of Lot 6 is out of range.", "... is in range.", "... was not scored.", "... could not be aligned." The tab reads "Lot 6, wafer 5 · ChamberWatch". The file name sits on the meta line in mono, beside the date, the role and warning words and the two links.
- Above the `h1`, the lot strip: the lot's wafers as 34px discs (24px on a phone), each a link to its run with its wafer number inside; this wafer 48px (36px on a phone, 32px at 360px and under) with an ink halo, `aria-current` and a THIS WAFER label. Its score is in its name and in the verdict beside the `h1`, not in the disc. The wafers come from the runs list for the run's source; the strip holds its height while that loads and is left out if it fails. The baseline follows the Runs page's rule for one lot: one bracket over the first N, or a centre dot in each baseline disc.
- Beside the `h1`, the verdict, only when the run has a score and a baseline: HOW UNUSUAL, a disc and the score in mono 72px (60px on a phone, the nearest step), one sentence, and a `ScoreLine` from 0 to four limits or the score, rounded up to 5 and stopped at ten limits like every score line. A rule on its left, no box.
- Fig. 1, the timeline: one 26px `--paper-2` lane per channel along the etch, excursions in solid red, a stuck value hatched in ink, the injected fault as a dashed ink line, a leader at the first moment, and the score at the right in mono 20px after a state dot (solid red past the limit, a ring under it). A run no channel left shows the closest one on a score line; a run caught only on a phase mean or spread lists those channels.
- A simulated run adds the injected fault as a figure whose answer is whether the detectors caught it.
- Fig. 2, the lineup: lollipops against one 2px limit line, a heavy red stem and dot past the limit, a thin stem and a ring under it, the ring kept 2px clear of the line as on a score line. The first ten by rank show, with every channel past the limit, the one with the largest score and every one that deviates or got stuck, wherever they rank; a button ("21 more channels, all under 3.0", `aria-expanded`) shows the rest in place and opens whenever the channel picked, in the address or from another figure, is one of them. On a short scale the 0 or the end label gives way when it would come within 8px of the limit's label. The open row is tinted `--paper-2` with a 4px inset ink bar. How the rank works sits in a closed disclosure.
- Fig. 3, the trace: the reading as an ink min-max envelope, redrawn in red inside each excursion, a red rail of excursion ticks above the plot (stuck values hatched in ink on the same rail), the band in `--paper-3` with a direct label. The Etch and Cycles views fit the y axis with `fitTraceDomain` in `charts/trace.ts`, from the first time slice where the reading sits inside a settled band (one at most four times the view's median width), and print one note for what they clip ("Below 70 until 41.4 s; starts at 6.9"). Whole record shows everything. Phase statistics and holds follow as tables, each titled above its sideways-scrolling box; every excursion sits in a closed disclosure. A view of a few cycles counts and ticks only the excursions inside it, and labels "First" only when the run's first moment is in view. A live region announces what the pointer or the arrow keys read.
- Label and Alignment are small panels: the rule and a 24px title, no number. Facts are a ruled list with mono labels. The Label panel shows its three buttons disabled while the settings load, and says the server is read-only only once the settings say so; if they fail, the buttons stay usable, since the server still refuses a read-only relabel. Alignment says what its numbers are: "198 found, none filled in where a marker was missing" for the phase starts, "41.1 s with no samples, at 648.8 s, outside the etch" for the largest gap, and a degraded run's note in the same words (`describePhaseStarts`, `describeGap` and `describeAlignmentNote` in `run.ts`).
- The trace's key, its keyboard hint and its table speak of time slices ("Reading, lowest to highest in each time slice"), a word a newcomer reads without a definition.
- The lineup and the trace share a row 11 to 13, so the lineup's name column (256px) holds the longest channel name and its DEVIATES or STUCK word on one line, and every row keeps one height; a row grows only when its words cannot fit.

## The Lots page
- The `h1` is the answer, from `lotsAnswer` in `lotSummary.ts`: "Lots 6 to 9 hold all 15 out-of-range runs.", with the lead "The other 6 lots ran 60 wafers with none out of range." It has sentences for one lot, one run, every lot, none, a source with no baseline ("None of the 10 lots has been scored yet.") and an empty source ("No lots yet."). When no lot is flagged, only the runs can say whether anything was scored: the answer waits for them, and says "No lot has an out-of-range run on record." if they fail.
- Fig. 1, Every lot, with the Source switch in its head: lot names in the serif at 20px, the date in mono (the Date and Conditioning columns only when some lot of the source has one, `lotColumns` in `lotSummary.ts`, so Simulated has neither), a Wafers column of `mini` discs from the source's runs list (one bracket over the column's head or centre dots by the baseline rule, a dotted divider after the baseline, a short dash for a slot the lot never ran), and the Out count as a 28px mono numeral that opens the lot's rows on the Runs page (a zero is 17px in `--ink-3`; on a phone both are 20px). The discs take no tab stop: the keyboard goes lot by lot, and each lot's page lists its wafers as links. A key under the table names only the marks it holds, and names the baseline by the dotted line ("Wafers 1 to 3, left of the dotted line, are the baseline runs."), which every width draws. While the runs load each row holds a placeholder; if they fail the column stays empty and one line in the key's place says "The wafers did not load." with Try again. On a phone each lot takes two lines, its name and count over its wafers at full size.
- Fig. 2, drift and depth by wafer position, as two `PositionChart`s with end labels; titled "Drift by wafer position" once its report is in and has no depth (Simulated), since then only the drift is drawn. The drift chart shades BASELINE only when the runs' baseline is each lot's first N wafers and N is the report's reference count; the depth chart shades FIRST 3. Its answer is the depth trend, or with no depth (Simulated) how many lots each position holds, from `describeRunsByPosition` in `lotSummary.ts` ("Wafers 4 to 10 come from one lot; wafers 1 to 3 average 11 lots."). The readout adds a Runs row, and a position holding fewer than half the lots of the fullest (`thinPositions`) is never annotated as the peak. While another source's report loads, the figure is busy and has no answer. "Show as a table" and the method sit on one line under them.
- Fig. 3, public wafers only: depth predicted from the telemetry, measured solid against predicted dashed; beside it how far each guess misses as three bars on one scale with the value at each bar's end, the worst drawn hollow, captioned with the reference count ("Wafers after the first 3"), its axis ticks printed with `tickFormat` (on a phone the 0 is set from its mark rightward, inside the gutter); the chart shades FIRST 3 and has "Show as a table" under it; one note line for the misses over every measured wafer; the five strongest features as a ruled list with mono values.
- The source line closes the page under a hairline.

## A lot's page
- Its lot strip over an `h1` in the run page's words, from `lotHeadline`: "Wafers 4 to 10 of Lot 6 are out of range.", "Every wafer of Lot 1 is in range.", "No wafer of Lot 6 has been scored yet." The lead, from `lotLead`, says how the rest divide when some were not scored or aligned, then what the drift verdicts judge, the fitted line: "By the last wafer, the line fitted through the lot's SF6 means is outside the band for 4 channels, and none is heading out." A lot too short to judge says "Too few wafers to judge a trend in the SF6 mean yet.", and a lot where only some channels are too short says how many. The meta line holds the date, the conditioning, the wafer count and the link to the runs table.
- Fig. 1 scores every wafer in the runs table's ledger: a disc and words for its state, its score on one shared line, its first channel and when it left. On a phone the wafer's name takes a fixed 88px column, so the discs line up row to row and never cover a name; the state's words wrap under the disc.
- Then the Phase and Reference switches over Fig. 2: the verdict counts as mono numerals (counting every channel), then each channel with a trend verdict (out of the band, heading out, staying in) and the picked one, in rank order, with its verdict as a word (`--drift` for out of the band or heading out, ink for the rest) and its Fitted z, the fitted line's z at the last wafer, `fittedZ` in `lotSummary.ts`. The channels with no trend or too few wafers fold behind a button in the lineup's style ("17 more channels, no trend", `splitDriftChannels` and `describeHiddenDriftChannels`), which opens when the picked channel is one of them. A closed "The words this page uses" follows: the two phases, Fitted z and z, the fitted line with its slope and t, and the verdict words, with the numbers from the lot's drift rule.
- Fig. 3, the picked channel's phase mean by position against its band and fitted line, its verdict word in the head and its wafers in a table that keeps each wafer's own z; the mean and slope columns follow the aligned-column rule (`alignedFormat` in `format.ts`). With the lot's own first wafers as the reference, the chart shades them under FIRST 3 (the reference count), as the Lots page's depth charts do, since they need not be the lot's baseline runs.

## The wafer page
- An `h1` from `waferHeadline` in `format.ts`, saying what it computes, the sites near the edge against those near the center: "Wafer 5 of Lot 6 etched deeper at its edge than at its center.", or the reverse, or "etched evenly from center to edge" when the difference Fig. 1's answer prints rounds to 0.00 µm; the mean when too few sites compare; "has no 9-point measurements" when the set is empty; "Wafer 5 of Lot 6" alone when the measurements fail, so no placeholder waits above the error. The file name sits on the meta line.
- Fig. 1, the wafer map on the ink ramp, the 89-point or 9-point switch in its head, its facts as `Stat` numerals and the radial profile on the same ramp. Fig. 2, public wafers only: the predicted depth as an ink dot with the lot's typical miss as a band around it and the measured depth as an ink tick, on one line. While another set's prediction loads, the strip dims, the figure is busy and has no answer.

## The Live page and the rest
- The Live `h1` says what the chamber is doing, from `liveAnswer` in `live.ts`: "A wafer is being etched now." with where it is, "Nothing is being etched." with what the last recording came to, or "The last recording stopped." With nothing recorded yet, the lead is the one next step, "Start the chamber simulator, then record from it here.", or on a read-only server "This ChamberWatch is read-only, so it does not record live runs."; until the settings say which, there is no lead. "How to start the chamber simulator" sits under the answer, open when it is the one next step: nothing recording, nothing stored, no failure, and a server known to record. Fig. 1, the chamber now, as `Stat` readings over the actions, real buttons (Record filled in ink, the others drawn in ink; the fault button says what it does, "Plant a fault: cut Gas 5 flow to 36%"); Fig. 2, the last wafer it recorded, with a link to its run. A read-only server has no buttons, and the figure says it is read-only only where the lead has not.
- While a wafer records, the page asks for the session again 500 ms after each answer arrives, not on a clock, so a slow server's answer is never cancelled by the next request. A screen reader hears the `h1` (or the failure's) through a visually hidden status; the lead's cycle and time change on every answer, so they are plain text, read when the reader reaches them.
- An address with no page says "Nothing is at this address." with a link back to the runs. A run, lot or wafer the API has no record of says so in the same pattern (see `PageFailure`).

## Spacing and shape
- Page width 1160px with a 24px gutter, 16px on a phone. Figures sit `--figure-gap` apart: 48px, 36px at 640px and under, on every page (the Lots page's figures sit in a `.sheet` like the other pages').
- The phone map shares the width out: `--pitch` is `min(26px, (100vw - 76px) / (positions + 1))` with the lot column 34px (2px of rounding slack in the 76px) and the lot's number alone (the link keeps "Lot 6" as its name), so ten wafers and the Out count fit from 320px. The Out head is set 0.06em tighter there, so "OUT" stays at least 6px clear of the last position's number. A lot longer than ten floors the pitch at 22px, scrolls inside its frame and pins the Out column to the right.
- Radius: discs and dots are circles; 6px on real controls (the theme button, buttons, selects) and the floating readout; 2px on bars and marks; nothing else is rounded.
- Shadows: none, except the chart readout, which floats. A disc's halo is an edge, not a shadow.
- The stylesheet keeps one `@media (max-width)` block per breakpoint at its end, widest first, so a narrower one wins where both apply.

## Motion
- One 180 ms fade with a 4px rise when a tab or source changes on the Runs page, and a 120 ms dim to half on a figure whose data is being replaced, until the new answer arrives. Nothing else moves. Both are off under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## Rules (every screen, always)
1. Placeholders that match the final layout for anything that loads data, so nothing jumps.
2. State is never carried by color alone: a solid disc with a number against a hollow ring against a dashed ring, and words next to marks.
3. No cards, chips, pills, tiles or boxed legends. No subtitle that restates a heading. A source line is not clutter.
4. Accessibility, WCAG 2.2 AA: 4.5:1 text contrast, 3:1 for marks and controls, full keyboard use with a visible focus ring on the paper and on the masthead, targets at least 24px (the one exception: the phone map's pitch falls to about 22.2px at 320px so all ten wafers and the Out count fit; each disc is still a link with the whole name), a label on every input, `prefers-reduced-motion` respected.
5. `frontend/src/designRules.test.ts` pins the radius, type scale, shadow, dark-token and token-only color rules against the stylesheet.
